# Shadows are stable cascades and pooled atlases with a budget, and skinned meshes skin once per frame in compute

ADR-0019 reserved the `Shadow` stage. This fills it, choosing current techniques over LearnOpenGL's single fitted map, 3×3 PCF and geometry-shader cubemaps, and it amends ADR-0035, because shadows draw a skinned mesh many times a frame.

## What casts

- **Lights opt in** through `castShadows`: on by default for the `DirectionalLight`, off for point and spot lights. Rect lights cast none (ADR-0048).
- **Meshes opt out** through `MeshRenderer`'s and `InstancedMeshRenderer`'s `castShadows`, on by default. `Masked` materials cast with their alpha cutoff; `AlphaBlend` and `Additive` ones never cast.
- **A budget, not a cap on lights.** `RendererConfig` sets how many point and spot lights may be shadowed (4 and 8 by default). The rest draw unshadowed, warned about once; which ones keep their shadows is chosen by distance to the view each frame.

## How

- **The sun uses cascaded shadow maps**, up to four per view (a cascade depends on the camera). Splits follow the practical scheme, a log and linear blend set by a lambda, and neighbouring cascades are blended across their boundary. Each cascade is a bounding sphere snapped to whole shadow-map texels, so shadows do not shimmer as the camera moves or turns.
- **Spot shadows share a 2D atlas; point shadows share a cubemap array.** Both are pools sized by the budget, not one texture per light.
- **No geometry shader for cubemaps.** Where the vertex stage can write `gl_Layer`, a point light's six faces draw as instances of one draw; otherwise as one draw per face.
- **Filtering is hardware depth comparison with Vogel-disk PCF**, rotated per pixel by interleaved gradient noise, for softer edges than a grid at the same tap count. Each light has a slope-scaled depth bias and a normal-offset bias. PCSS and VSM were set aside: PCSS can come later without any change users see.
- **Shadow passes instance** like every other pass (ADR-0051).

## Compute skinning

With the prepass, cascades and cubemap faces, one skinned mesh can be drawn ten times a frame. A compute pass at the start of the frame skins each skinned mesh once into a per-frame vertex buffer from ADR-0035's palette buffer, and every pass then draws that buffer rigidly. The `SKINNED` vertex variant stays for user shaders and as the fallback with compute skinning off. Palette upload, deduplication and the skip cases are unchanged.

*Amended while building lighting ticket 14 (2026-11-01):*
- **The pass.** `Skinning`, in a `RenderStage::Skinning` of its own between `Prepare` and `Shadow`, is the device's first compute pass: `builtin:skinning` (a compute program; FenrirShaderCompiler and `fenrir_add_shaders` take a `COMPUTE` stage) runs one dispatch per skinned mesh and palette range in the frame, one invocation a vertex. It reads the mesh's own vertex buffer as words and writes model-space positions, normals and tangents, three streams a mesh, into a skinned vertex buffer ringed per frame in flight. Passes that draw from it declare it as vertex input, and the OpenGL backend puts a memory barrier after a compute pass that writes storage.
- **Which shaders.** The built-in mesh programs' skinned draws are compute-skinned (`builtin:lit`, `builtin:unlit` and the fallback, told by their embedded bytes): their variant without `SKINNED` draws the skinned streams. A user shader's skinned draws keep its own `SKINNED` variant, since it may do more than skin; a user shader without the key falls back to the fallback material as before, which is compute-skinned. Every `SKINNED` variant draws when `RendererConfig::computeSkinning` (or `Renderer::SetComputeSkinning`, for comparing the two) turns compute skinning off, or when the compute pipeline cannot be made.
- **Instancing.** Compute-skinned items merge (ADR-0051) only when they read the same skinned vertices, one mesh on one skeleton, so a crowd draws one draw a member where vertex skinning merged it into a handful. Each draw is then a rigid draw of a few streams, and the shadow passes this was for draw each skinned mesh many times.
- **The maths.** `Fenrir/SkinMaths.glsl` holds the blend, the cofactor normal and the tangent's re-orthogonalisation for both paths. The compute path makes the tangent perpendicular to the normal in model space, before the skeleton entity's world matrix, so under a non-uniformly scaled skeleton entity it can differ from the vertex path's slightly, as any rigid mesh's tangent frame does.

*Amended while building lighting ticket 15 (2026-11-01):*
- **A shadow atlas per camera.** A camera's cascades sit side by side in one transient `Depth32Float` texture, each `RendererConfig::shadowMapSize` square (2048 by default; `shadowCascades`, 4, and `shadowSplitLambda`, 0.75, beside it), drawn by one `ShadowCascades` pass per view in the `Shadow` stage, a tile at a time. No texture arrays, so the device gains only a comparison sampler (`SamplerDesc::compare`).
- **The sun's members.** `DirectionalLight` gains `castShadows` (on), `shadowDistance` (100: how far from each camera the cascades reach; there is no far plane to stop at) and its two biases. A cascade's map holds its sphere with a texel to spare on each side, so snapping its centre never pushes the slice off the map, and catches casters up to `shadowDistance` past the sphere towards the light.
- **Biases at the receiver.** Both biases are in the cascade's own texels and move the shaded point before the lookup: `shadowNormalBias` along its normal, `shadowSlopeBias` towards the light per unit of slope (tan of the light's angle, held between 0.1 and 8). Nothing goes in the rasteriser's depth bias, so a light's bias needs no pipeline of its own and fits every cascade's texel size.
- **Casting and reading.** A shadow pass runs each casting item's own vertex stage behind `builtin:shadow`'s depth-only fragment stage, as the depth prepass does, both faces, a `Masked` item cut out by its prepass mask (a Masked material whose shader has no `baseColour` or `baseColourTexture`, which the prepass cannot cut out either, casts whole). `Opaque` and `Transparent` read the atlas at set 1 binding 2 and the cascades in the view block, through `Fenrir/Shadows.glsl`: 16 Vogel-disk taps 1.5 texels across, the last tenth of each cascade's range blended into the next. A camera's `debugView` can tint its view by cascade or show its atlas.

*Amended while building lighting ticket 17 (2026-11-01):*
- **The budget's distance.** The spot lights that keep their shadows are those whose position is nearest any camera of the frame, one choice for every view: a frame has one spot shadow atlas, not one a view. Spot lights asking past the budget are warned about once.
- **One atlas the renderer keeps.** The spot shadow atlas is a `Depth32Float` texture kept across frames, a grid of the budget's tiles (ceil(sqrt(budget)) across), `RendererConfig::spotShadowMapSize` (1024) each, made again when the budget changes (`Renderer::SetSpotShadowBudget`), and drawn by one `SpotShadows` pass in the `Shadow` stage. It belongs to the frame, so set 0 reads it: the records at binding 7 (`FenrirSpotShadow`: world to the atlas, the biases, a texel's world size one unit along the light, the tile's bounds) and the atlas at binding 8. The `SpotShadows` pass binds a set 0 group with the map that lights everything there, so it never reads what it draws.
- **The light buffer's order.** The shadowed spot lights come first in the light buffer, in tile order, and the frame block's `lights.y` counts them, so record i < `lights.y` reads spot shadow i and the 80-byte record is unchanged.
- **The tile's projection.** A square perspective from the light along its axis, wide enough for the outer cone (held to 80 degrees) with the filter's reach and a texel to spare inside the tile, its near plane a two-hundredth of its range, at most 0.1. Each spot light has its own two biases (`SpotLight::shadowNormalBias` and `shadowSlopeBias`), applied as the sun's are, in texels whose world size grows with the distance along the light. Past the tile, as for a cone wider than 80 degrees, a point is unshadowed.

*Amended while building lighting ticket 18 (2026-11-02):*
- **The budget and the cubes.** Point lights are chosen as spot lights are, within `RendererConfig::pointShadowBudget` (4), and each kept is a cube of one `CubeArray` texture the renderer keeps (`Depth32Float`, a cube per budget, `pointShadowMapSize` 512 square faces), made again when the budget changes (`Renderer::SetPointShadowBudget`). The device gains the cube array kind, depth attachments of one layer or of every layer at once, and `DeviceFeatures::vertexLayer`. Set 0 reads it: the records at binding 9 (`FenrirPointShadow`: the biases and a texel's world size one unit along a face's axis) and the cubes at binding 10; the shadowed point lights follow the shadowed spot lights in the light buffer, the frame block's `lights.z` counting them. Point lights gain their own two biases, as spot lights did.
- **What a face holds.** The distance from the light over its range, reversed (1 at the light, 0 at the range), written by `builtin:shadow`'s `POINT_SHADOW` fragment variant from the fragment's place in its face and its depth, so the range is the far plane and a receiver compares its own distance whichever face it reads, with the same comparison sampler, Vogel disk (laid across the way from the light) and receiver-side biases as the atlases. The cube array's faces filter into each other, so no seam needs margins.
- **Six faces without a geometry shader.** Where a vertex stage can write `gl_Layer`, a shader with a `POINT_SHADOW` key gets, from `Fenrir/Draw.glsl`'s `CurrentDraw`, each item as six instances, each turning the item's world matrix about the light into its face and choosing its layer; the item's own vertex stage runs unchanged, so it lands as in every other pass. Every built-in mesh program has the key. One `PointShadows` pass attaches every layer and draws each light's casters in one draw a run. Casters whose shader lacks the key, and all casters where the device cannot write `gl_Layer`, are drawn by a `PointShadowFaces` pass a face, through a view block of that face's own.
