# 04: Slopes, stairs and sticking to the ground

**What to build:** A character walks a level without tuning: up a ramp but not a cliff, up and down stairs without ever becoming airborne, sticking to the ground going down, and standing still on walkable slopes unless told to slide (spec: "the move in `PostTick`" (slopes, stairs, ground sticking, the trim rule), "ground state"; spike lessons 1 and 2).

- **Live fields wired up:** `maxSlope` (velocity into a too-steep slope cancelled), `stepHeight` (stairs), `floorSnap` (sticking to the ground walking down) and `stopOnSlope`, each switched off by 0 (or `false`), each applied at the next move. Finer stair tuning stays private.
- **The full trim rule on slopes:** on walkable ground with `stopOnSlope` an up-facing contact takes all of the falling part; otherwise only its component into the surface, so the rest slides the character downhill. Walking velocity is never trimmed along an up-facing contact's normal (that turned walking into climbing in the spike). Stair step-ups and ground snaps never show in `velocity`.
- **All four ground states:** `OnSteepGround` (steeper than `maxSlope` underneath) with the ground fields set, and `Touching` (something touched, nothing underneath) with them empty.
- **Rounded edges:** a ground contact's steepness is judged by the surface normal of the face it touches, not the contact normal, so a box's rounded edge over a walkable face is walkable ground; `groundNormal` reports that normal. If Jolt's ground state cannot be fed the face normal, say so in this ticket and ask before falling back to accepting the flicker.
- **DemoApp:** the 30° ramp, the 55° slope, ten 0.3 m stairs to a 3 m ledge; key 1 toggles `stopOnSlope`, 5 toggles stairs and ground snapping; the autopilot climbs the stairs and walks the ramp.

**Blocked by:** 03 (Character tracer bullet).

**Status:** resolved (built test-first on branch `claude/character-slopes-stairs-2b1a69`). Where the build settled something the ticket left open:

- **Rounded edges: Jolt's ground state could not be fed the face normal, so FenrirPhysics judges the ground itself from Jolt's contacts.** Jolt builds each contact's surface normal inside its collectors and hands listeners only `const` contacts, and on a box's rounded edge it replaces the face normal with the contact normal once that faces up more (the side face's normal is horizontal), so the edge reads as steep. The backend's `GroundOf` takes Jolt's ground state and supporting contacts after each move and judges each contact by `FaceNormal`: the touched shape's face facing up (`GetSupportingFace`) when the contact lies on it or within its rounding (Jolt's default convex radius, the most FenrirPhysics rounds by), it faces up more than Jolt's surface normal, and the contact normal is not horizontal (a contact met from the side is a side face). Ground Jolt reads as steep is `OnGround` when any supporting contact's face is walkable; `groundNormal` averages the faces' normals as Jolt averages its own; the ground fields come from the most upward-facing contact. The same judgment decides riding, the trim's steepness and `onWall`. This is not the flicker fallback: going down the stairs reads `OnGround` every tick with a normal of (0, 1, 0), where Jolt alone gave `OnSteepGround` with a normal of about (-0.76, 0.65, 0) (the test fails without it). Jolt's own stair walking and stick-to-floor still use its surface normals, which they only need for "supported".
- **`stopOnSlope` had to reach the move, not only the write-back.** The tracer bullet trimmed `velocity` after the move, but the move itself still carried the tick's gravity into the slope, which Jolt's solver turned into 0.08 m/s down a 30° slope (0.16 m in 2 s). On walkable ground with `stopOnSlope` the falling part of `velocity` is left out of the move as it is left out of the write-back; the character now holds within 1 mm.
- **Velocity into a too-steep slope is cancelled by Jolt's solver** (its steep-slope constraints): an explicit cancel of the horizontal part into steep ground changed nothing in the tests and was dropped.
- **`Touching` with `onWall`:** the wall test compared each contact with Jolt's ground body, which Jolt sets to the deepest contact even when nothing supports the character, so a character falling against a ledge read `Touching` without `onWall`. The ground contact is now only the supporting one.
- **How a slide reads:** with `stopOnSlope` off, the trim rule turns each tick's falling part into a downhill part that is then kept as horizontal velocity (walking velocity is never trimmed along an up-facing normal), so `velocity` stays nearly horizontal and grows at about g·tan(slope) while the move follows the face. The tests assert a real slide (over 4 m/s after a second on 30°, moving along the face), not g·sin(slope). Game code that sets its horizontal speed every tick creeps (under 0.5 m/s), as the spike found; this is in `CharacterBody`'s doc comment.
- **Walking up a slope:** Jolt walks the horizontal speed along the face, so 2 m/s up 30° is 1.5 m/s along x, with no upward speed in `velocity`.
- **Turning `stepHeight` back on** at the foot of a 0.3 m step lifts the character 0.12 m on the next tick and onto the step within a few ticks (Jolt's step-up from a standstill against the riser).
- **DemoApp:** in `CharacterScene.hpp`, `BuildSlopesAndStairs` puts a ledge 3 m high at x = -16 to -20 in the far west, reached by a 30° ramp (z = -1.5) and ten 0.3 m steps 0.5 m deep (z = -6), with a 55° slope leaning on it (z = 5). `SlopesAndStairsLeg` comes first in the route, so the smoke autopilot walks it: up the ramp (standing halfway for a second), down the stairs, up them, down the ramp, and a running jump onto the steep slope, which reads on steep ground and slides back down. In the Debug smoke run the ramp and the stairs gave no ground-state change at all. Keys 1 (`stopOnSlope`) and 5 (`stepHeight` and `floorSnap` together) are in the smoke keys.
