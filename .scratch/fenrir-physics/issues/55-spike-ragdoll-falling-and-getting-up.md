# Spike: a character that falls as a ragdoll and gets up, through the proposed model

Type: prototype
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 51, 52, 53, 54

## Question

Do the collision groups from [How do general collision groups work?](51-general-collision-groups.md), the model from [How is a ragdoll modelled in the ECS, and where does the glue live?](52-ragdolls-in-the-ecs.md), the description and builder from [From what description is a ragdoll built, and with which bodies, joints, motors and strengths?](53-ragdoll-description-bodies-joints-and-motors.md) and the modes from [How does a ragdoll switch between following and simulating, and how is it powered?](54-ragdoll-switching-powering-and-following.md) work in practice? Build a throwaway spike on a `prototype/fenrir-physics-ragdolls-spike` branch, in its own worktree: a skinned walking character (the AnimationDemo's Quaternius human or CesiumMan, tick-posed) whose ragdoll is built from a description, following its bones as kinematic hit boxes; a key drops it into simulating (with a push), another makes one arm limp, another powers it towards the walk, and another gets it up with a blend back; boxes to fall onto and a second ragdoll to collide with; a character body under the skeleton that the ragdoll ignores. Made through the proposed public interface with Jolt behind the seam, in the demo scaffold (a PhysicsDemo scene, or the AnimationDemo if physics is easier to add there). Check it by hand, as the earlier spikes were.

React to it: does the interface read well; does a derived ragdoll stand and fall believably with default limits and masses; do the hit boxes follow the walk with no lag and the right velocity; does the switch pop or fly; does a limp arm hang from its kinematic shoulder without stretching; does a powered ragdoll hold a walk pose and recover from a shove; does the skeleton entity follow the hips and does getting up blend back without a jump; do the two ragdolls collide with each other but not with themselves or their characters; is it deterministic (two runs bit-identical); does anything about Jolt leak. Record the lessons the spec needs.

The spike's scene may later become the C++ ragdoll demo scene (see "What do the ragdoll demo scenes show?").

## Answer

**Verdict: the model of tickets 51 to 54 works end to end through the seam, and nothing about Jolt reaches FenrirRagdoll or the demo.** The user ran PhysicsDemo scene 7, "Ragdolls (spike)", on 2026-11-03: the fall looks believable with the default limits and masses ("it just goes limp"), the walk's root-motion glitch is gone once the demo pins the root bone, and "the ragdolling seems to work". Two findings needed changes (lessons 8 and 9). The limp arm, the get-up jump, two ragdolls colliding and the hit boxes under F2 were not reported on either way; the headless checks cover the first three.

Prototype (primary source, never merged): branch `prototype/fenrir-physics-ragdolls-spike` (three commits, head d983b77f, cut from `main` d563ac82). The hand-over note `ragdoll-55-spike.md` in the sessions' shared notes folder has the full report. The scene: a tick-posed Quaternius human walking a lane on a `CharacterBody`, both in one collision group, its 16-capsule, 15-joint ragdoll following as hit boxes; Space drops it (`Simulating`, weights 1, a 120 N·s shove, the character disabled), A makes the left arm limp and blends it back over 1 s, K powers it with 1 and 2 stepping `strength`, G gets up (`Following`, a 1.5 s weight ramp, the character teleported under the skeleton entity); a bystander human in its own group, crates, a wall, readouts in names, P autopilots. The slice covers `CollisionGroup` in FenrirPhysics (a body-to-group table in the joints' `JoinedPairs` filter, the character's inner body and move filtered by the same rule, 3 tests), and `packages/FenrirRagdoll` with `Ragdoll`, `RagdollBody`, `RagdollMode`, `HumanoidBones`, `RagdollPart`, `RagdollDescription`, `DescribeHumanoid`, `BuildRagdoll`, `DestroyRagdoll` and the `Tick`/`Update` glue (5 cases, 1590 assertions, `[ragdoll-spike]`; two smoke runs print identical readouts). Not built: C#, the API description, scaling, offset recalibration, self-colliding ragdolls, root drive, target velocities.

**Where it guessed or skipped:** the default numbers are the spike's picks (below), not the research tickets'; capsule radii are fractions of the stature (head-to-foot rest distance), not of bone length, because torso bones are too short; forward is where the feet point; leaf parts run to the bone's first child or along the parent for 0.10 to 0.13 × stature; the character capsule is a sibling of the model root; the second ragdoll is dropped, not steered; no per-scene `CheckSceneLog.cmake` entry.

**Lessons the spec needs** (1, 2 and 9 amend ADR-0054; 3 to 6 amend ticket 53 and ADR-0053):

1. **The switch does not keep the kinematic move's velocity by itself.** A body that becomes dynamic gets the velocity its `RigidBody` component holds, and physics writes velocities back for dynamic bodies only, so the glue writes `linearVelocity` and `angularVelocity` from its last two following poses each tick.
2. **Following the hips in full position puts the skeleton entity under the floor** (hips rest height about 0.97 m, body lying at 0.09 m). The glue follows horizontally and keeps the entity's height.
3. **Sixteen parts, not fifteen:** hips, spine, chest, head and three per limb.
4. **`strengthScale` lives on `RagdollBody`**, read per tick, not only on the description part.
5. **The description carries each joint's rest frame and anchor** (`jointFrame`, `jointAnchor`, model space): the hinge and twist axes cannot be derived at build time from `JointKind` alone.
6. **`BuildRagdoll` needs the asset store** for the rest pose: `BuildRagdoll(App&, Scene&, Entity, description)`.
7. Unmapped spine bones between mapped ones keep their animated local pose under the overridden parent, as ADR-0038 says; fine in the log.
8. **Locomotion clips with root translation slide the hit boxes** and snap at the loop (no root-motion extraction, ADR-0034). The demo pins the root bone at its rest translation in a hosted `Animate` item after the sampler; the spec's demo does the same, uses an in-place clip, or extracts root motion.
9. **Powering towards a cycling locomotion clip is the wrong target:** the legs step on the ground, since the motors track the walk's base pose each tick (the user saw it on K). The overrides themselves take on all 16 bones (`[demo-rig]` check). Game code pauses or swaps the clip when it powers the ragdoll, and the spec says so; a pose source for `Powered` other than the base pose is the demo-scenes ticket's question.

**Default numbers used** (the spec's starting values): radius as a fraction of stature: hips 0.065, spine 0.055, chest 0.06, head 0.055, upper arm 0.025, lower arm 0.022, hand 0.02, upper leg 0.038, lower leg 0.03, foot 0.025, capsule half-height length/2 − radius (min 0.005). Mass fractions of 70 kg: hips 0.14, spine 0.12, chest 0.22, head 0.08, upper arm 0.03, lower arm 0.02, hand 0.01, upper leg 0.10, lower leg 0.045, foot 0.015 (per side). Swing-twist limits (cone half-angles Y = Z / twist ±): spine and chest 20°/20°, head 30°/45°, shoulder 80°/45°, wrist 35°/20°, hip 45°/25°, ankle 30°/10°; hinges (elbow, knee) 0 to 140° on the bend side. Joint friction 0.5 N·m; motors off until powered; `maxTorque` = strength × strengthScale × 30 N·m per kg of the child body, spring frequency lerp(1 Hz, 10 Hz, strength), damping 1. Shove 120 N·s on the chest; get-up ramp 1.5 s; arm blend 1 s.
