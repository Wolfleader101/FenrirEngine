# How do other engines build and drive ragdolls?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: none

## Question

How do other engines put ragdolls in front of game code, from their docs and source? Survey Unity (the Ragdoll Wizard, `CharacterJoint`, `Rigidbody` per bone, Animator and ragdoll blending, active ragdolls through `ConfigurableJoint` drives, and the Unity Physics / DOTS ragdoll sample), Unreal (the Physics Asset and its editor, `SetAllBodiesBelowSimulatePhysics`, `SetAllBodiesBelowPhysicsBlendWeight`, `PhysicalAnimationComponent` with its profiles and strengths, `RigidBodyAnimNode` in the animation graph), Godot (`Skeleton3D`'s "Create physical skeleton", `PhysicalBone3D`, `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`, `physical_bones_start_simulation` with a bone subset), Jolt's own samples (as the backend's reference; the facts are in "What does Jolt offer for ragdolls?", so only the API shape here), and Bevy (bevy_rapier or Avian ragdoll examples or crates, if any). For each:

- **The description:** what a ragdoll is built from: a hand-authored asset (Unreal's Physics Asset), a wizard that derives bodies from named bones and lengths (Unity, Godot), code; how bodies are named and matched to bones (bone name, bone index, a node per bone); whether the description is per skeleton asset or per instance; what tooling generates it and how good the result is without hand tuning.
- **Bodies:** which bones get bodies in the usual humanoid (hips, spine, chest, head, upper and lower arms, hands, upper and lower legs, feet: 11 to 15), which shapes (capsules, boxes, spheres), how the body is placed relative to the bone (offset, along the bone to its child), and how mass is spread.
- **Joints:** which joint kinds join parent and child (Unity `CharacterJoint`: a swing-twist with asymmetric limits; Unreal: a six-DOF with cone and twist; Godot: cone or hinge per `PhysicalBone3D` joint type), what limits the wizards set, and whether elbows and knees are hinges or cones.
- **Collision inside and around the ragdoll:** how parts of one ragdoll avoid colliding with each other (parent–child only, overlap-based pairs at rest pose, Unreal's per-body collision toggles in the asset), and how a ragdoll avoids its own character controller or capsule (Unity's layer matrix and `Physics.IgnoreCollision`, Unreal's collision profiles and `IgnoreActorWhenMoving`, Godot's `add_collision_exception_with`): layers, groups or per-pair ignore lists, and what game code does when one character's ragdoll must still hit another's.
- **Switching:** how a character goes from animated to ragdoll (kinematic bodies following bones then set dynamic, bodies only created at death, `SetSimulatePhysics` per body), whether it is per body, per subtree or all at once, and how the initial velocity is carried over.
- **Blending:** how the simulated pose reaches the skeleton (a blend weight per body or per bone, Unreal's `PhysicsBlendWeight`, Godot's `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` influence), and how a character gets up (a blend back over time, a get-up animation matched to the final pose, snapping the character root to the ragdoll's hips).
- **Powered ragdolls:** how motors are driven towards an animated pose (Unreal's physical animation profiles with strength and position and velocity gains; Unity drives on `ConfigurableJoint`; Godot's none), what a strength setting is in physical terms (spring, damping, max force), and whether the drive is in local joint space or world space.
- **Hit boxes:** whether hit detection uses the same bodies as the ragdoll (kinematic while animated) or separate shapes, and how hits are reported with a bone name.
- **The character's root:** what happens to the character's entity, capsule and camera while the ragdoll is live (follow the hips, stay put, teleport on get-up).
- **ECS fit** (Unity DOTS, Bevy): which components, how a ragdoll body references its bone and its ragdoll, and how the update fits a system.
- **Scripting access**, and **common complaints** (jitter, limbs stretching, self-collision jams, ragdolls flying away on switch, get-up pops, tuning joints by hand).

Put the findings on a `research/engine-ragdoll-models` branch, in `.scratch/fenrir-physics/research/engine-ragdoll-models.md`, and link them here.

## Answer

Findings: branch `research/engine-ragdoll-models`, `.scratch/fenrir-physics/research/engine-ragdoll-models.md` (commit e4188617): one section per engine (Unity's wizard and `CharacterJoint`, the Unity Physics DOTS ragdoll sample, Unreal's Physics Asset and `PhysicalAnimationComponent`, Godot's `PhysicalBone3D` and `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`, Jolt's samples, Rapier, bevy_rapier and Avian, which ship none, and the third-party bevy_ragdoll), a comparison table, and the options for FenrirPhysics with no choice made. Pinned to commits and versions; Unreal from its 5.8 manual and 5.4 API pages (source needs a login).

- **Description:** a wizard writing components onto bone objects (Unity: 13 slots auto-filled from the humanoid avatar, saved as a prefab), an editor command adding one `PhysicalBone3D` per non-leaf bone keyed by `bone_name` with a `body_offset` (Godot), a hand-authored asset per skeletal mesh generated from skinned vertices and keyed by bone name (Unreal's Physics Asset), a settings object one-to-one with a skeleton's joints plus a name-matching `SkeletonMapper` (Jolt), code (Unity Physics, Rapier), or a profile generated from any skeleton through a bone-name alias table (bevy_ragdoll). Unity's and Godot's wizards size capsules from bone-to-child length only; all expect hand tuning.
- **Bodies:** 10 to 16 per humanoid (Unity 13 with no hands or feet, Unity Physics 16, Jolt 12, Rapier 10, bevy_ragdoll 16, Godot one per non-leaf bone pruned by hand). Capsules along the bone (Unity radius 0.2 to 0.3 × length, Godot 0.1 ×), torso boxes or a hull, head sphere. Mass: Unity density weights (torso 2.5, legs 1.5, arms and head 1.0) scaled to a total; bevy_ragdoll a segment table rescaled to 80 kg; Godot 1.0 each.
- **Joints:** Unity `CharacterJoint` (swing-twist with asymmetric twist; wizard hips −20/70/30, knees −80/0/0, spine −20/20/10, arms −70/10/50, elbows −90/0/0, head −40/25/25); Unreal one six-DOF with swing and twist each free, limited or locked; Godot pin by default ("crumples"), cone or hinge by hand; Jolt swing-twist everywhere; Unity Physics two cone joints per ball joint and limited hinges for elbows, wrists, knees and ankles; Rapier spherical plus revolute elbows and knees. Elbows and knees are hinges by kind in Unity Physics, Godot and Rapier, and zero-swing swing-twists in Unity, Jolt and Unreal.
- **Collision:** jointed pairs only (Unity, Godot, Rapier), one group per ragdoll so no part hits another (Unity Physics negative group index, Jolt `GroupFilterTable`, bevy_ragdoll's mask), or per-body toggles all off by default (Unreal). Against the character's own capsule: layer matrix plus `IgnoreCollision` (Unity, not persistent), profiles plus `IgnoreActorWhenMoving`, which does not cover simulating bodies (Unreal), layers or collision exceptions (Godot), a unique group id per ragdoll (Jolt). Ragdoll against ragdoll collides by default everywhere.
- **Switching:** kinematic bodies following animation flipped per body (Unity `isKinematic`, Godot), per bone subtree (Unreal `SetAllBodiesBelowSimulatePhysics`, Godot `physical_bones_start_simulation([names])`), or whole ragdoll (bevy_ragdoll's `RagdollMode { Animated, Kinematic, Dynamic, Frozen }`; Jolt sets the motion type at creation). Velocity: carried by kinematic bodies (Unreal), explicit (Unreal, Jolt, Unity Physics), captured from animation (bevy_ragdoll, Unreal's RigidBody node); none in Unity or Godot.
- **Blending:** none in Unity (the bodies are the bones); a weight per body written into the pose (Unreal `PhysicsBlendWeight`, subtree setters); one `influence` per simulator after the animation mixer (Godot); one blend per character (bevy_ragdoll); game code copies poses (Jolt). Getting up: Unreal's pose snapshot held and blended into a get-up montage; game code everywhere else.
- **Powered:** Unity `ConfigurableJoint` drives (target rotation, spring, damper, max force, local unless configured in world space); Unreal `PhysicalAnimationComponent` profiles per body (orientation, angular-velocity, position and velocity strengths, max forces, a global multiplier) plus joint motors from animation; Jolt `DriveToPoseUsingMotors` in local joint space on swing-twist and hinge only; bevy_ragdoll motors scaled by a 0 to 1 strength plus world-space pins; Godot none.
- **Hit boxes:** the same bodies, kinematic while animated, everywhere; the bone reported by name (Unreal `BoneName`), by the hit object or node (Unity, Godot), or by body entity and index (bevy_ragdoll).
- **Root:** no engine moves the controller; the capsule stays put, and the recurring bugs are the controller colliding with its own ragdoll and a capsule chasing the pelvis; Jolt offers `GetRootTransform`.
- **ECS fit:** bodies and joints are plain entities (Unity Physics: joint and body-pair entities, a group index the only tie, no skeleton); bevy_ragdoll adds body-of-character relationships, per-body kind, joint-to-parent, drive target and no-contact mask, and per-character mode, drive, weights, blend and target pose, with capture and write-back around transform propagation and fixed-rate drive sets.
- **Scripting and complaints:** everything is component data or callable verbs. Complaints: stretching (Unity projection and mass scale, Jolt locked translations), jitter from self-collision and mass ratios (Unity's 2:1 advice, Unreal mass halving), flying off at the switch from overlapping the controller or activating mid-step, get-up pops when blending back by weight alone (Unreal), descriptions rotting when authored against a non-rest pose (Godot, Unity's captured frames), modifier order breaking ragdolls (Godot), Rapier lacking coupled angular motors.
- **Against the decided tickets (51 to 54, 56):** consistent. `RagdollBody` on body entities naming a bone is Godot's and bevy_ragdoll's shape; one group per ragdoll is Jolt's, Unity Physics's and bevy_ragdoll's; per-body mode with inheritance covers Unreal's and Godot's subtree switching and bevy_ragdoll's whole-ragdoll mode; hinges at elbows and knees match Unity Physics, Godot and Rapier; strength as a 0 to 1 scalar is bevy_ragdoll's and Unreal's multiplier; ADR-0038's per-bone weights are already finer than any engine's. Worth the spec's attention: Unity's wizard limits as a second source of defaults beside Jolt's; the mass-ratio advice (2:1) agrees with Jolt's `Stabilize`; descriptions must be derived from the rest pose, never the current pose (two engines' open bugs); the controller colliding with its own ragdoll is the bug ticket 51's group exists to prevent.
