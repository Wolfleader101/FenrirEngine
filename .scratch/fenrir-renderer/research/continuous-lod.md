# What continuous level of detail would take for FenrirRenderer

The engine developer asked, after ADR-0057 settled discrete per-mesh LODs chosen by projected error, per-view GPU frustum culling into indirect draws and two-phase Hi-Z occlusion: what would continuous LOD (meshlet or cluster hierarchies, Nanite-style "virtual geometry") take beyond that, whether and when to go there, and what it costs. This file answers that for FenrirRenderer as it stands. It decides nothing: the decision belongs to an ADR that would amend ADR-0057.

Read on 2026-11-06. The engine is read at `fenrir-renderer-lighting` @ `2b9cb165` (the device headers and the GL backend) and at `claude/culling-lod-spec` @ `5594cf0d` (ADR-0057 and the culling spec). Everything else is read upstream, at the URL given with each claim. One source could not be read: Brian Karis's SIGGRAPH 2021 slide deck, *A Deep Dive into Nanite Virtualized Geometry* (Karis, Stubbe, Wihlidal; [course page](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/index.html), [PDF, 16 MB](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)), because the fetch tool stops at 10 MB. Where a Nanite fact below comes from a secondary source that cites the deck, it says so. Unreal's source needs an Epic account and was not read.

## The short answer

Continuous LOD is a different asset, a different cull pass and, in its full form, a different way of shading. What ADR-0057 plans is the first third of it, and most of that third carries over:

- **The asset.** A mesh becomes a DAG of clusters of about 128 triangles, each level built by grouping neighbouring clusters, simplifying the group with its border locked, and splitting the result back into clusters, with a bounding sphere and an error per group that never shrinks up the DAG. meshoptimizer now ships this as a single header, `clusterlod.h` (MIT, in the library FenrirAssets already plans to take as a submodule), with a monotonic error, an enclosing-sphere rule, attribute-seam protection and a BVH over the groups. It builds; it does not select, stream or draw. Building is slow enough that it belongs in a cooked format: the only published figures put a 1.64 billion-triangle scene at 2 min 35 s on 16 cores, which is roughly a second per million triangles per core.
- **The cull.** The per-view compute pass of ADR-0057 stays, and gains a second level: after an instance survives, its clusters are tested one by one (frustum, Hi-Z, and a cut rule of "my error is under a pixel and my parent's is not") and the survivors are written as draw commands. That needs two device additions on top of the culling spec's ticket 01: `DispatchIndirect` and an indirect draw count read from a buffer. Both are OpenGL 4.6 core and Vulkan 1.2 core.
- **The shading** is where Nanite and Bevy part from a forward renderer. Both rasterise clusters into a 64-bit visibility buffer (depth in the high word, cluster and triangle id in the low) with atomic max, a compute software rasteriser for clusters under about 64 pixels and the hardware for the rest, and then shade with one fullscreen pass per material. That needs 64-bit image atomics (a Vulkan extension; on OpenGL a GLSL extension whose driver exposure could not be verified), storage images, and a material model with no user vertex stage. It cannot run under MSAA. None of that fits ADR-0024 and ADR-0049 as written.

So the honest split is: a **minimum viable CLOD** that keeps the forward frame, the user vertex and fragment programs, MSAA and the existing passes, by drawing surviving clusters as ordinary indexed draws through the indirect path, is about four tickets of device and cull work on top of the culling spec plus a cooked-asset decision, and buys no popping, one asset for every distance, per-cluster occlusion and a cap on triangles per pixel. What it does not buy is Nanite's micro-triangle efficiency: hardware rasterisation of one-pixel triangles pays the quad overshading tax, which is why Nanite wrote a software rasteriser. The full form, with a visibility buffer, is a second renderer. The recommendation at the end is to finish ADR-0057 first, add LOD cross-fade if popping is what hurts, and take the minimum viable form only when a scene of large, dense, static meshes shows discrete levels are not enough.

## 1. What continuous LOD is, concretely

**Vocabulary.** A *cluster* (Unreal) or *meshlet* (NVIDIA, meshoptimizer, Bevy) is a run of up to 64 to 256 triangles sharing a small vertex set. A *group* is a handful of neighbouring clusters simplified together. The *DAG* is the structure of groups across levels: a cluster's parent is the group it was split from, and a group's children are the clusters it was built from; it is a DAG rather than a tree because grouping and re-splitting cross the old cluster boundaries. The *cut* is the set of clusters drawn in a frame: one per surface point, chosen by projected error.

**Nanite's pipeline, as the secondary sources describe the deck.** Clusters are 128 triangles and are "the only renderable part" (Rob Wyatt, [Nanite Deep Dive, Part 1](https://trickybitsblog.github.io/2024/04/20/nanite.html), citing UE's `NaniteClusterCulling.usf` and `NaniteHierarchyTraversal.ush`; a Stack Exchange answer cited by the same searches says a DAG "of 128-triangle clusters" is built per mesh at import). At runtime a BVH over the groups is traversed by a pool of worker threads fed from "a Multiple Producer, Multiple Consumer job queue" (Wyatt, same page), which is the persistent-threads scheme; traversal stops when a node is culled, when its triangles are small enough, or when its children are not resident. Occlusion is two-phase against a hierarchical Z-buffer: a main pass against last frame's HZB reprojected with last frame's transforms, and a post pass "identical to the main pass" against an HZB built from this frame's depth; instance tests pick the mip where "the screen rect is < 4x4 pixels" (Wyatt). Rasterisation splits per cluster "on the fly based on the projected area of the cluster": a compute software rasteriser in which "a thread-group of 128 compute threads, renders the 128 triangles of a single cluster at the same time", which Wyatt estimates at "maybe 3x or 4x faster for small triangles", and the hardware for large ones with "only the visibility buffer is bound, no traditional depth buffers or frame buffers". Both write a 64-bit visibility buffer, "32bits are used by depth and 32bits are used to identify the triangle" (Wyatt); Scthe's notes on Wihlidal's GDC 2024 talk give the write as 64-bit atomics of `depth << 32 | clusterId` ([Notes from 'Nanite GPU Driven Materials'](https://scthe-github-io.vercel.app/blog/nanite-materials-notes)). Shading then runs one fullscreen triangle per material: material ids are written to a depth buffer so "the depth compare hardware can be used to cull/accept pixels before the pixel shader even runs" (Wyatt), and since UE 5.4 pixels are instead binned into per-material lists and shaded by indirect compute dispatches, "1 thread == 1 pixel" (Scthe, citing Wihlidal slides 59 to 130). Cluster pages stream on demand from GPU feedback, and "the lowest LODs are always present in the GPU memory" (Wyatt).

**The error and the cut rule.** The deck "is a bit vague about the metrics used to switch between LODs" ([jglrxavpok, Recreating Nanite: runtime LOD selection](https://jglrxavpok.github.io/2024/04/02/recreating-nanite-runtime-lod-selection.html)). The open implementations all converge on the same rule, which that post states as: the cluster is rendered if `clusterError <= errorThreshold && parentError > errorThreshold`, with the error projected through the group's bounding sphere and the threshold in pixels, 1 by default. Three properties make the rule correct and parallel: the error must grow monotonically up the DAG (else clusters with `parentError == selfError` "will never be rendered"), each cluster's decision must depend only on its own and its parent's values (so every cluster can decide alone), and "meshlets of the same group must have the same error and bounds to make the same LOD decision", else the cut has holes or overlaps (same post). meshoptimizer's header states the same requirement from the other side: "it is incorrect to use the precise bounds of the merged or simplified mesh, because this may violate monotonicity" ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)).

**What is essential and what is Unreal's.** Essential to continuous LOD: the DAG with group-level monotonic error and enclosing spheres, the per-cluster cut rule, per-cluster frustum and occlusion culling, and drawing clusters rather than meshes. Unreal's choices on top: 128-triangle clusters (meshoptimizer and Bevy use the same number, NVIDIA's ray-tracing path prefers 64 to 128), the persistent-threads traversal (Bevy dispatches once per BVH level instead; NVIDIA's sample turns its persistent kernel off on other vendors' hardware, see §2), the software rasteriser and the visibility buffer (both exist to make one-pixel triangles cheap and to shade once per pixel; a forward renderer can skip them at a cost described in §7), the material-depth and shading-bin machinery (a consequence of the visibility buffer), and page streaming (a consequence of scenes too big for VRAM).

## 2. Open implementations, by what their code does

### meshoptimizer and `clusterlod.h`

**Versions.** `meshopt_partitionClusters`, `meshopt_buildMeshletsFlex` and `meshopt_computeSphereBounds` arrived as experimental in v0.23 (14 March; the releases page shows no years, and the dates below are inferred from the order: [v0.23 notes](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/releases/tag/v0.23)). v0.24 (12 June) made `meshopt_partitionClusters` take vertex positions "as an optional input" for spatial merging and added `meshopt_buildMeshletsSpatial` for ray tracing ([v0.24 notes](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/releases/tag/v0.24)). v1.0 (8 December) added `clusterlod.h`, "a new single-header library" that "implements continuous level of detail by generating a hierarchy of clusters", made Flex and Spatial stable, and promised API, ABI and behaviour compatibility for everything but permissive simplification ([v1.0 notes](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/releases/tag/v1.0)). The latest is v1.3 (25 September), which adds border dilation to `clusterlod.h` for foliage and says permissive mode is now stable ([releases](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/releases)). The licence is MIT, with a request for attribution in user-facing documentation ([README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)).

**The building blocks** ([README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)):

- `meshopt_buildMeshlets` balances vertex reuse against culling efficiency and is "recommended for offline data processing even if cone culling is not used"; `meshopt_buildMeshletsFlex` takes `min_triangles`, `max_triangles` and a `split_factor` (2.0 recommended) and "favors spatial locality over full meshlets", which "suits hierarchical LOD"; `meshopt_buildMeshletsSpatial` clusters by a surface-area heuristic for ray tracing.
- `meshopt_computeMeshletBounds` gives a bounding sphere and a normal cone; `meshopt_computeSphereBounds` computes the sphere alone.
- `meshopt_partitionClusters` "groups clusters into partitions of similar size, prioritizing topological and spatial locality"; partitions can exceed the target, up to "target + target / 3", and the README says they "can support hierarchical simplification similar to Nanite". This is the METIS replacement.
- `meshopt_simplifyWithAttributes` takes attribute weights and a per-vertex lock; `meshopt_simplifyScale` converts the normalised error to object space; the options `meshopt_SimplifyLockBorder`, `meshopt_SimplifySparse` ("intended for meshlet simplification"), `meshopt_SimplifyErrorAbsolute`, `meshopt_SimplifyPrune`, `meshopt_SimplifyPermissive` (collapses across attribute discontinuities except protected vertices) and `meshopt_SimplifyRegularize` are the ones a DAG builder uses.

**What `clusterlod.h` does** ([source](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)). It calls itself "a small "library"/example built on top of meshoptimizer to generate cluster LOD hierarchies", to use as-is or as a reference. Input is a `clodMesh`: indices, positions, optional attributes with weights, an optional per-vertex lock and an `attribute_protect_mask` whose bit K marks attribute K as a seam to protect. `clodDefaultConfig(max_triangles)` (asserted between 4 and 256) sets `max_vertices = max_triangles`, `min_triangles = max_triangles / 3`, `partition_size = 16` (a target, "actual partitions may be up to 1/3 larger"), `simplify_ratio = 0.5`, `simplify_threshold = 0.85` (a group that keeps more than 85 % of its triangles is "stuck" and emitted as terminal), permissive simplification on with a sloppy fallback at `simplify_error_factor_sloppy = 2.0`, and `optimize_clusters` on. The build loop is: position-only remap and seam protection; clusterise (Flex, or Spatial under `cluster_spatial`); then while more than one cluster remains, partition with `meshopt_partitionClusters`, lock vertices shared between groups, simplify each group toward half its triangles (configured mode, then permissive, then sloppy), emit the group with its error made monotonic by `max(previous_error * simplify_error_merge_previous, current_error) + current_error * simplify_error_merge_additive`, its bounds as the merged enclosing sphere of its children, and re-clusterise the output with `refined` pointing at the group. Errors are absolute, in mesh units (`meshopt_SimplifyErrorAbsolute`). It is single-threaded and needs no METIS.

**What it hands the engine.** Per `clodCluster`: `refined` (the group that produced it, or −1 for original geometry), `bounds`, and `indices` into the original vertex buffer; per `clodGroup`: `depth` and `simplified` bounds (sphere plus error). The header gives the projection to use at runtime, `bounds.error / max(distance(bounds.center, camera_position) - bounds.radius, camera_znear) * (camera_proj * 0.5f)`, a 0..1 value to multiply by the screen height, noting it ignores perspective distortion; and the rule: render a cluster "if its group's `simplified` error is over the threshold, and either `refined` is -1 or `groups[refined].simplified` is at or under the threshold". It warns that `clodCluster::bounds` "should only be used for culling, as bounds.error is not monotonic across DAG". `clodBuildHierarchy` builds a forest with one tree per DAG level, nodes of `node_width` children with merged bounds holding the "worst case error for all groups in the subtree", for a traversal that descends "into nodes while bounds is over error threshold".

**What it does not do:** no runtime selection beyond the formula and the rule, no rasteriser, no streaming, no serialisation, no GPU code. `demo/nanite.cpp` is a harness over it: it builds with `clodDefaultConfig(128)`, projects errors with a threshold of `2e-3f` ("2 pixels at 1080p"), simulates a cut through the BVH, and dumps it as OBJ; "there is no rasterization or runtime LOD selection beyond the simulated cut" ([nanite.cpp](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/nanite.cpp)). The author's blog adds that "the runtime code is responsible for streaming and rendering them to minimize the visual error" and that a hierarchical acceleration structure for selection was, at the time of writing, done in `vk_lod_clusters` "with existing meshopt_ functions" ([Billions of triangles in minutes](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)).

### Bevy's virtual geometry (`bevy_pbr::experimental::meshlet`, by JMS55)

**Build** ([from_mesh.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/from_mesh.rs)). Input must be an indexed triangle list with exactly `{POSITION, NORMAL, UV_0}`; tangents "are calculated at runtime and are not stored in the mesh". Triangles are partitioned into meshlets with METIS (`partition_count = triangle_count.div_ceil(126)`, "Need to undershoot to prevent METIS from going over 128 triangles per meshlet"), then `build_meshlets(…, 256, 128, 0.0)`; groups of `TARGET_MESHLETS_PER_GROUP = 8` are also cut with METIS, weighted by shared vertices; borders are locked; each group is simplified with `simplify_with_attributes_and_locks` (normals as attributes, `Sparse | ErrorAbsolute`, no error cap) toward half its indices, and a group that keeps over 60 % (`SIMPLIFICATION_FAILURE_PERCENTAGE`) is "stuck"; `parent_error` is forced to be "atleast as large as all of its constituent meshlet's individual errors", spheres are merged, and `verify_bvh` asserts both are monotonic. The 0.16 post says clustering moved from `meshopt_buildMeshlets` to METIS because "DAG quality is the most important part of virtual geometry" ([Virtual Geometry in Bevy 0.16](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-03-27-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-16/)). METIS is Apache 2.0 ([LICENSE](https://github.com/KarypisLab/METIS/blob/master/LICENSE)). Positions are quantised to 1/16 cm by default and bit-packed per meshlet, normals octahedral in `pack2x16snorm`, UVs uncompressed.

**Asset** ([asset.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/asset.rs)). `MeshletMesh` holds packed vertex streams, `indices`, a BVH8 ("The root is at index 0"), `meshlets`, `meshlet_cull_data` (per-meshlet AABB and LOD sphere), the mesh `aabb` and `bvh_depth`, which "is used to determine the number of dispatches at runtime". It is saved with lz4 as `.meshlet_mesh`, format version 3, and "The conversion step is very slow, and is meant to be ran once ahead of time, and not during runtime".

**Runtime** ([mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/mod.rs), [cull_clusters.wesl](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/cull_clusters.wesl), [cull_shared.wesl](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/cull_shared.wesl), [visibility_buffer_software_raster.wesl](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/visibility_buffer_software_raster.wesl)). Shaders: `cull_instances`, `cull_bvh`, `cull_clusters`, `visibility_buffer_software_raster`, `visibility_buffer_hardware_raster`, `meshlet_mesh_material`, `resolve_render_targets`, `remap_1d_to_2d_dispatch`, `fill_counts`, `clear_visibility_buffer`. The LOD test is `lod_error_is_imperceptible`: for perspective, `norm_error = simplification_error / max(d, near) * projection[1][1] * 0.5` with `d` the distance to the sphere's surface, passing when `norm_error * view.viewport.w < 1.0`, and the file cites meshoptimizer's `nanite.cpp` line 115. Clusters are frustum-tested by AABB and occlusion-tested against a Hi-Z whose mip is chosen so the footprint is about 4×4 texels, taking the minimum of 16 samples ("sampling 16 times a finer mip is worth the extra cost"). The same entry point is compiled twice for the two occlusion passes; clusters occluded in the first are queued from the right end of a shared buffer for the second. Routing: `sw_raster = all(aabb_size <= vec2<f32>(64.0))`. The software rasteriser is one 128-thread workgroup per cluster, up to 256 vertices in workgroup memory, one triangle per thread, scanline or bounding-box fill, and writes `textureAtomicMax(meshlet_visibility_buffer, …, (u64(depth) << 32u) | u64((cluster_id << 7u) | triangle_id))`; fixed-point maths and the top-left rule are "not yet implemented". Material shading is one fullscreen triangle per material with a depth-equality test, and the 0.14 post warns "Each fullscreen triangle will cost an entire screen's worth of depth comparisons" ([Bevy 0.14 post](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/)).

**Requirements and gaps.** `required_wgpu_features` are `TEXTURE_INT64_ATOMIC`, `TEXTURE_ATOMIC`, `SHADER_INT64`, `SUBGROUP`, `DEPTH_CLIP_CONTROL` and `IMMEDIATES`; "This plugin currently works only on the Vulkan and Metal backends"; it exits if MSAA is on ("This plugin is not compatible with Msaa"); `cluster_buffer_slots` must not exceed 2^25 ([mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/mod.rs)). Materials "have no control over the vertex shader or vertex attributes", must be opaque ("Transparent, alpha masked, and transmissive materials are not supported"), must avoid derivative built-ins and UV arithmetic, and must implement `Material::meshlet_mesh_fragment_shader` or its prepass and deferred variants ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/pbr/experimental/meshlet/struct.MeshletMesh.html); [material.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/material.rs), where each is "part of an experimental feature"). The input attribute set excludes joints and weights, so skinning is out by construction. Streaming, BVH culling, compression and tooling are open in the tracking issue ([#11518](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/11518)), and the 0.16 post says "culling is the biggest bottleneck at the moment". Performance it reports: 3,375 bunnies of 144,042 triangles each at LOD 0, visibility buffer only, 0.93 ms on an RTX 3080 at base clocks at 2240×1260 (4.97 ms before the software rasteriser); a bunny is 4.5 MB in memory and 3.61 MB on disk with a 12-level DAG ([Bevy 0.15 post](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)).

### Godot 4

Godot has no meshlets and no continuous LOD. It generates discrete LODs at import: "The meshoptimizer library is used for LOD mesh generation behind the scenes", automatically for glTF, .blend, Collada and FBX scenes (not OBJ unless imported as a scene), with the caveat that "The mesh LOD generation process is not perfect, and may occasionally introduce rendering issues". Selection "uses a screen-space metric" from the AABB point nearest the camera, against a global `mesh_lod_threshold` of 1 pixel "tuned to look perceptually lossless", with a per-instance `lod_bias`; every instance of a MultiMesh shares one level ([Mesh level of detail](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)). Hand-made levels and HLOD go through *visibility ranges* on any `GeometryInstance3D`, with fade modes Disabled (hysteresis, instant), Self (alpha fade, "doesn't force rendering to become transparent" is why Disabled is fastest) and Dependencies; "Godot currently only supports alpha-based fading", and dithered cross-fade is a documented workaround through per-material distance fade ([Visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html)). That is ADR-0057's design plus a fade.

### NVIDIA: `vk_lod_clusters`, `nv_cluster_lod_builder`

`nv_cluster_lod_builder` was a "continuous level of detail (LOD) mesh library" over `nv_cluster_builder` and meshoptimizer's `simplify`, Apache 2.0, with the same shape as clusterlod (groups cut for minimum boundary, halved triangles, quadric error, spheres enlarged to bound generating geometry, "the angular error, or whatever metric is compared to a threshold, must never decrease with each level of decimation"). Its README now says the repository "has been archived and is no longer maintained by NVIDIA" and points to meshoptimizer's `clusterlod.h` ([nv_cluster_lod_builder](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder)). It called itself "a quick placeholder or learning tool" and did not plumb attributes through.

`vk_lod_clusters` is the rendering sample: it "makes use of the meshoptimizer library to process the model and generate the required cluster and LoD data", through "a lightly modified local copy" of `clusterlod.h`, streams geometry from RAM to VRAM with every operation "performed indirectly on the device", and draws either with `VK_EXT/NV_mesh_shader` or ray traces with `VK_NV_cluster_acceleration_structure` (driver 572.16 or later); "The persistent kernel is disabled on non-NVIDIA hardware" ([vk_lod_clusters](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters)). Its rasterisation path is mesh shaders only, so without `VK_EXT_mesh_shader` the sample does not rasterise. Apache 2.0.

### Others, briefly

- **nanite-webgpu** (Scthe) builds the DAG in the browser "using WebAssembly for meshoptimizer and METIS", culls per instance and per meshlet with a previous-frame depth pyramid, and software-rasterises; its FAQ lists "No two-pass occlusion culling", "No visibility buffer. It's not possible with the `atomic<u64>` limitation that I have", no streaming, no compression, and "Meshlet simplification is.. simplistic". Numbers: a 250k-triangle dragon reduces to 102 triangles at one root meshlet; a 1.7 billion-triangle scene has "Nearly 98% of the triangles software rasterized" ([README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu)).
- **niagara** (Kapoulkine) is a streamed Vulkan renderer whose episodes cover cluster cone culling with task shaders, draw compaction with `KHR_draw_indirect_count` and LOD, depth-pyramid occlusion, meshlet occlusion culling and cluster compute culling; it is discrete per-mesh LOD over meshlets, not a DAG, and needs the Vulkan 1.4 SDK ([README](https://github.com/zeux/niagara)).
- **jglrxavpok's Carrot engine** write-up is the clearest statement of the cut rule and its three preconditions (§1).

## 3. What it needs from the graphics device

### What FenrirRenderer has and lacks

Read at `fenrir-renderer-lighting` @ `2b9cb165`: `Device/CommandList.hpp` has `CmdDraw`, `CmdDrawIndexed`, `CmdDispatch` and `CmdCopyBuffer`, with `ResourceUsage::StorageRead/StorageWrite/VertexInput` and a `Compute` queue kind; `Device/Resources.hpp` has `BufferUsage::Storage`, `TextureUsage::Storage` (declared but no command or binding uses it), formats up to `Rgba32Float` and `Rg16Float`, four binding sets, and `DeviceFeatures { vertexLayer }`; `OpenGLDevice.cpp` dispatches with `glDispatchCompute` and issues `GL_SHADER_STORAGE_BARRIER_BIT | GL_VERTEX_ATTRIB_ARRAY_BARRIER_BIT` after a pass with `StorageWrite` uses. The culling spec's ticket 01 adds `BufferUsage::Indirect`, `ResourceUsage::IndirectRead`, `CmdDrawIndexedIndirect { buffer, offset, drawCount, stride }` over `glMultiDrawElementsIndirect`, and `GL_COMMAND_BARRIER_BIT` (`.scratch/fenrir-renderer-culling/issues/01-indirect-draws-in-the-graphics-device.md`).

| Mesh and task shaders | `GL_NV_mesh_shader` only, NVIDIA contributors only, requires GL 4.5 ([spec](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/NV/NV_mesh_shader.txt)) | `VK_EXT_mesh_shader`, ratified, contributors NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Arm, Qualcomm, Valve, needs 1.2 or `VK_KHR_spirv_1_4` ([refpage](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VK_EXT_mesh_shader.html)) | no | no | optional (Nanite and Bevy use none; `vk_lod_clusters` uses them) |
| Subgroup operations | `GL_KHR_shader_subgroup`, an ARB-numbered extension not in core, and a future core version "should add support for all of SPIR-V 1.3" ([spec](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/KHR/KHR_shader_subgroup.txt)); FenrirRenderer compiles SPIR-V 1.0 for GL | core 1.1 | no | no | wanted (Bevy requires `SUBGROUP`; persistent-thread queues and scanline raster use them) |
| Bindless textures | `GL_ARB_bindless_texture`, not core ([spec](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/ARB/ARB_bindless_texture.txt)) | descriptor indexing | no | no | wanted for compute material shading |
| Persistent threads | no API; a work queue with forward-progress assumptions | same | — | no | Nanite yes; Bevy no (one dispatch per BVH level); NVIDIA sample "disabled on non-NVIDIA hardware" |

Three things follow. The minimum viable form needs two small device additions (indirect count and dispatch indirect), both core in GL 4.6 and Vulkan 1.2, in the shape ticket 01 already set. The full form needs 64-bit image atomics and storage images, which on GL are an extension whose availability across the three desktop vendors this note could not confirm, plus subgroup operations that need a SPIR-V version the GL path does not emit (the Slang study notes the same SPIR-V 1.0 constraint for `gl_Layer`). And mesh shaders are not on the critical path of any of the three software implementations, so a GL-only engine loses nothing by not having them.

## 4. What it means for user shaders

In a visibility-buffer pipeline the material is not a vertex and fragment program over a mesh. The rasteriser runs a fixed vertex transform, writes ids, and the material runs later as a fullscreen or binned pass that reconstructs the triangle from the ids, interpolates attributes itself and computes its own derivatives. Bevy says it plainly: "Materials have no control over the vertex shader or vertex attributes", derivatives are forbidden and so is manual UV arithmetic, and a material supplies a `meshlet_mesh_fragment_shader` instead ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/pbr/experimental/meshlet/struct.MeshletMesh.html)). Unreal keeps the material graph but constrains it: "Nanite supports materials that have their Blend Mode set to Opaque and Masked", world position offset "is supported but limited" and must be clamped to bound cluster culling, "Deformation with Morph Targets is not supported", custom expressions "might result in artifacts", vertex interpolators "will be evaluated three times per pixel", and the whole feature excludes "Forward Rendering, stereo VR, MSAA, Lighting Channels" ([Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)). The Programmable Rasterizer added in 5.1 for Fortnite is Epic's way of letting material-graph logic (WPO, masks) back into the raster stage through binning ([Epic tech blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4)).

Against ADR-0024 ("A user's shader is a full program. Vertex and fragment stages on the engine includes") and ADR-0049 ("a G-buffer cannot run ADR-0024's user fragment programs, and it breaks ADR-0019's MSAA"), a visibility buffer is the same rejection twice: the user's vertex stage is gone and MSAA is gone. The forward path can coexist with cluster LOD only in the minimum viable form, where a surviving cluster is an ordinary indexed draw over the mesh's own vertex streams. Then `Fenrir/Vertex.glsl`'s locations, `CurrentDraw()` through the indirection buffer, `SKINNED` and `ALPHA_MASK` variants, the depth prepass with its equality test, MSAA and the shadow passes all hold, and the user shader cannot tell a cluster draw from a mesh draw. A CLOD mesh is then a mesh whose index buffer holds every DAG level and whose draw list is written by the GPU per view, drawn through the same pipelines. The two kinds of mesh go through one path at the material level and two at the cull level, which is what ADR-0057 already does for lone items and instance lists.

## 5. The asset pipeline

**Build cost.** The only first-party figures are for the whole Zorah scene, "1.64B triangles, 18.9B triangles with instancing", "36.1 GB on disk": the author's baseline with 16 threads on a Ryzen 7950X was "~9m 20s, using ~54.6 GB RAM", and after sparsity, sorting, SIMD and allocator fixes it reached "~2m 35s" on Linux and 2 min 38 s on Windows, "around 3.5x speedup from our initial baseline" ([Billions of triangles in minutes](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)). That is about 10 million input triangles per second across 16 threads, including file I/O, so a one-million-triangle mesh is on the order of one to two seconds of a single core with the current header (an estimate from those figures, not a measurement; the header is single-threaded, and the parallelism in that post is across meshes). Bevy's `from_mesh` "is very slow, and is meant to be ran once ahead of time" with no number given ([asset.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/asset.rs)). Wyatt describes Unreal's build as "a complicated and quite slow process" and "not practical at runtime". For LightingDemo's asteroid ring (rocks of 3.3k to 6.8k triangles, a few distinct meshes) a load-time build would be milliseconds per mesh and fine; for a 1M-triangle statue or a scanned environment it is a load hitch per mesh and the argument for a cooked format, which ADR-0057 already defers to "its own ADR, and when it comes, this is what it cooks".

**Size.** With `simplify_ratio = 0.5` the DAG's index data is a geometric series, about twice LOD 0 across all levels (arithmetic, not a citation); meshoptimizer's meshlet codec shrinks indices "by around 3x" in `vk_lod_clusters` ([README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters)). Bevy's 144k-triangle bunny is 4.5 MB in memory and 3.61 MB on disk with packed positions ([0.15 post](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)). Per cluster the runtime needs a sphere and an error for its own group and its parent group (two `vec4`), its index range, and a parent link: about 48 bytes per 128 triangles. Nanite notes that quantised positions must share an exponent across levels, "up to 21 bits per axis", per the meshoptimizer README.

**Streaming.** Nanite streams cluster pages on GPU feedback with root pages resident; `vk_lod_clusters` streams from RAM to VRAM with device-side requests; Bevy keeps "All asset data … in memory" with streaming as future work ([0.14 post](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/)). Streaming is what makes a scene larger than VRAM possible; it is not what makes continuous LOD work, and a Fenrir version can begin resident, as Bevy's does. Under ADR-0057's line it is also partly the game's: which meshes exist is the game's streaming, which levels of a mesh are resident could be the renderer's.

**What the format must hold.** The mesh's vertex streams (unchanged, so skinning and user vertex layouts keep working), one index buffer holding every cluster of every level, per cluster `{firstIndex, indexCount, group, refinedGroup, cullSphere}`, per group `{sphere, error, depth}`, optionally the BVH. `clusterlod.h` is deterministic for a given input and version, so a build-time tool and a load-time build give the same bytes; the cooked format's job is to skip the time, not to change the answer.

## 6. Skinning and animation

- **Unreal.** Nanite Skeletal Mesh exists and is documented with "A Skeletal Mesh API", "one draw call per mesh", Virtual Shadow Map shadowing and "No geometry LODs. Nanite Skeletal Mesh uses animation LODs" ([Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)); Epic's 5.5 preview announcement says "Nanite can now render Skeletal Meshes incredibly fast (currently an Experimental feature)" ([forum post by Epic](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unreal-engine-5-5-preview/2048423)). The same page's deformation section says "Deformation with Morph Targets is not supported with Nanite", and forum reports (not Epic statements) add cloth. The 5.5 and 5.6 release-note pages were fetched but the Nanite skeletal section was not in the readable portions, so the exact 5.6 status is **unverified** here. How Nanite skins clusters (whether bounds and error are evaluated on the bind pose) was not readable without the source.
- **Bevy** requires exactly `{POSITION, NORMAL, UV_0}`, so a skinned mesh cannot be converted; skinning is not on the tracking issue ([from_mesh.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/from_mesh.rs), [#11518](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/11518)).
- **meshoptimizer** simplifies indices over the caller's vertices and knows nothing about joints; a skinned mesh's clusters simply index the same skinned streams.
- **FenrirRenderer** skins in compute into a per-item vertex buffer that later passes read as `VertexInput` (ADR-0050, lighting ticket 14), and ADR-0057 already says "A skinned mesh's levels share its skin" and uses the skinned bounds of ADR-0035. The minimum viable form inherits that: cluster draws read the skinned buffer like any draw. What changes is the error: the group spheres are in bind space, so a skinned item's cluster test should scale the sphere by the same factor the skinned bounds use, which keeps it conservative, and a mesh whose animation is much larger than its bind pose (a long rope) will pick detail by the wrong distance. Godot documents the same weakness for its discrete levels ("skinned meshes are especially affected"). Cluster-level culling of a skinned mesh is exact only if the cluster spheres are re-fit per frame, which no implementation surveyed does.

## 7. Judgement, for the ADR author

### What a minimum viable CLOD in FenrirRenderer would be

1. **Asset.** `MeshData` gains an optional cluster DAG beside, or instead of, `lods`: one index list for all levels, a cluster table and a group table, built by a `GenerateClusters(const MeshData&, const ClusterSettings&)` in FenrirAssets over `clusterlod.h` with the UV seams protected by `attribute_protect_mask`, as ticket 06 does for `GenerateLods`. At load until a cooked format exists.
2. **Cull.** The per-view pass of tickets 03, 04 and 11 keeps its instance level and gains a cluster level: a second dispatch, sized by `DispatchIndirect` from the survivors, with one thread per (surviving copy, cluster); it tests the cut rule with the header's projection against `RendererConfig::lodErrorPixels`, then the cluster's sphere against the frustum and, under `occlusionCulling`, against the shared depth pyramid, and appends a `DrawIndexedIndirectCommand { indexCount, 1, firstIndex, baseVertex, indirectionEntry }` with an atomic counter. The pass's `drawCount` comes from that counter through an indirect count. Shadow views select by the camera as ADR-0057 says.
3. **Draw.** `DepthPrepass`, `Opaque` and the shadow passes issue one multi-draw per (pipeline, material, mesh) run of cluster commands, exactly as they will for discrete levels, so `CurrentDraw()` and every user shader are untouched.
4. **Device.** `CmdDrawIndexedIndirectCount` and `CmdDispatchIndirect`, with an `IndirectRead` use on the count buffer; GL 4.6 and Vulkan 1.2 both have them core. Nothing else.

No software rasteriser, no visibility buffer, no material resolve, no persistent threads, no streaming, no mesh shaders. Four tickets on top of the culling spec, plus the cooked-format ADR when load time demands it.

### What it buys over ADR-0057's discrete levels

- **No popping and no level count to pick.** The cut moves one cluster at a time under a one-pixel error, so there is no visible switch and no `maxLevels`/`ratio` to tune; Godot's docs and the cross-fade workaround in §2 show what discrete levels need instead.
- **Triangle density is capped everywhere on the mesh**, not per mesh: a large mesh near the camera keeps detail where it is close and sheds it where it is far, which a per-mesh level cannot do. This is the case that discrete levels lose outright: terrain-sized or building-sized meshes.
- **Per-cluster culling.** The back half of a building and the far side of a rock are culled by Hi-Z and frustum at cluster granularity, which ADR-0057's per-copy test cannot, and which matters most for the same large meshes.
- **One asset for all distances**, with the level data generated once and used by every view.

### What it costs

- **Hardware rasterisation of pixel-sized triangles.** With the error at one pixel the drawn triangles are a few pixels across, and the GPU shades them in 2×2 quads, so most fragment invocations are helpers; this is the overshading Nanite's software rasteriser exists to avoid, and Bevy routes every cluster under 64 pixels to compute for the same reason. Under ADR-0049 the depth prepass is cheap (no fragment work) but `Opaque` runs the full user fragment shader with the equality test, and the helper invocations still run. The practical mitigation is a larger `lodErrorPixels` for cluster meshes (meshoptimizer's demo uses two pixels at 1080p), which trades a little silhouette fidelity for shading cost. This is the honest limit of the minimum viable form, and no cited source measures it for a forward renderer.
- **Draw command volume.** One command per visible cluster means hundreds of thousands of 20-byte commands per view for a dense scene (Bevy's bunny scene has 3,375 instances × up to 1,125 clusters before culling). GL's multi-draw loops over them in the driver; how fast each vendor's driver does so was not found in any primary source and would need measuring. The alternative, Nanite's single draw with vertex pulling by `gl_VertexID`, removes the per-command cost and the index buffer, but replaces the vertex stage, which is the user's under ADR-0024.
- **Memory**: about twice the index data per mesh and 48 bytes per cluster, resident.
- **Build time** at load for big meshes, or a cooked format.
- **Testing.** The null backend cannot know the cut; GL tests must read the command buffer back, as the culling spec already plans for levels, and compare the cluster set against a CPU walk of the same DAG with the same formula. `clusterlod.h` is deterministic, so a committed small mesh gives a stable oracle.
- **Two mesh kinds.** A mesh either has `lods` or clusters; the cull pass has an instance branch and a cluster branch; `RendererStats` counts both. The surface area is bounded because nothing above the cull changes.

### What the full form would add, and why it is a second renderer

A software rasteriser and visibility buffer need storage images with 64-bit atomics (§3), kill MSAA, replace the user vertex stage, and move shading to a per-material resolve that cannot share `Fenrir/Lighting.glsl`'s forward loop as it stands. Unreal and Bevy both reject forward rendering and MSAA for it. If FenrirRenderer ever wants it, it is after a Vulkan backend (for `VK_EXT_shader_image_atomic_int64` and subgroups), after a cooked format, and as a separate opaque path that ADR-0049 explicitly declined to write twice.

### The alternative most games ship

Discrete levels with a cross-fade. ADR-0057 lists "LOD cross-fading" under "Not yet", and Godot's visibility ranges, with dithered fade as the documented cheap form, are the production version of it. For the scenes LightingDemo has (100k small rocks, a city of repeated facades) discrete levels chosen by projected error already remove the triangle cost, because a rock is one or a few clusters at any distance that matters; continuous LOD pays off on meshes that are big on screen and dense at once, and those scenes do not exist in the demos yet. Popping is the one defect discrete levels keep, and a dithered cross-fade in the prepass and `Opaque` (a per-instance fade factor in the draw record, `ALPHA_MASK`-style discard) fixes it for a ticket's work, with no asset change.

## Recommendation

1. **Finish ADR-0057 as specified.** Everything in it is a prerequisite for either path: the per-view compute cull, the indirection buffer, indirect draws, the Hi-Z pyramid and projected-error selection are exactly what a cluster cull extends. Nothing in the culling spec should change for this.
2. **Shape ticket 01 so the two later commands fit**: a buffer-sourced draw count and an indirect dispatch are the same pattern (`Indirect` usage, `IndirectRead`, a command struct the GL backend replays and the null backend checks). They can be their own small ticket when needed; they do not have to be in 01.
3. **Shape ticket 06 so cluster data could sit beside `lods`.** `MeshLod { indices, error }` over the mesh's own streams is already the right vertex-sharing shape; a cluster table is a further set of index ranges over the same streams. No change needed now, only not foreclosing it.
4. **Add LOD cross-fade before cluster LOD** if popping is the complaint: cheaper, asset-free, and what Godot and most shipped games do.
5. **Take the minimum viable form only on evidence**: a scene with large dense static meshes (a scanned environment, terrain-sized props, a hero statue) where discrete levels measurably either pop or cost too many triangles up close. When that scene exists, the ADR is: `clusterlod.h` through FenrirAssets, a cluster level in the cull pass, two device commands, a cooked format, hardware raster only, forward shading unchanged, `lodErrorPixels` likely above one for cluster meshes.
6. **Do not plan the visibility-buffer form on OpenGL.** It needs 64-bit image atomics and subgroups that the GL SPIR-V 1.0 path and the extension landscape do not reliably give, and it contradicts ADR-0024 and ADR-0049. Revisit when a Vulkan backend exists.

### What could not be verified

- The Nanite deck itself (fetch limit); its figures above are second-hand from Wyatt, Scthe and jglrxavpok, who cite it and Unreal's source.
- Whether desktop GL drivers from AMD and Intel expose `GL_EXT_shader_image_int64`; only the GLSL extension text was read.
- The exact status of Nanite Skeletal Mesh in UE 5.6 and later; only Epic's 5.5 "Experimental" statement and the current documentation page were read.
- Per-vendor throughput of `glMultiDrawElementsIndirect` with hundreds of thousands of commands, and the shading cost of one-pixel triangles under a forward fragment shader; both need a measurement, not a citation.
- `clusterlod.h`'s single-mesh build time on this engine's meshes; the Zorah figures are the only published ones and include I/O and serialisation.
